What Is a Digital Garden

A digital garden is a personal site organised by ideas and links rather than by dates. Notes grow over time.

🌳 Evergreen Apr 22, 2026 · Updated Apr 26, 2026 · 1 min read
digital-garden concepts writing

A digital garden is a personal website organised around ideas and the links between them, rather than around chronology. A blog is a stream — newest at the top, posts finished and forgotten. A garden is a network — notes are added, revised, cross-linked, and allowed to mature in public.

Three things tend to be true of digital gardens:

  1. Notes are small and atomic. One idea per page. Titles are claims or concepts, not headlines.
  2. Notes link densely to each other. A reader can wander between related ideas without going through a navigation menu.
  3. Notes have visible maturity. Half-formed thoughts are allowed and labelled as such — see [[Note Maturity Stages]].

The metaphor is older than the web. A garden is cultivated, but never finished. It is a place you walk through, not a feed you scroll. Kavu (കാവ്, Malayalam for sacred grove) leans into the metaphor.

A real one in the wild

The Kavu theme was built for, and is used by, stultus.in/notes — the digital garden of Hrishikesh Bhaskaran. Browsing it is the easiest way to see what the theme looks like with a few hundred densely cross-linked notes covering software, philosophy, and storytelling.
